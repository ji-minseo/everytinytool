Lossy vs Lossless Compression Explained With Examples

By Every Tiny Tool Editorial · Published October 20, 2026

Compare lossy and lossless image compression, learn what each method preserves, and choose practical settings without confusing file size with image quality.

Open the image tools ↗

What lossy compression actually discards

Lossy encoders reduce stored data by approximating or removing information in a picture. JPEG and lossy WebP typically offer substantial savings for photographs because not every subtle variation must be stored exactly. At very low settings, smooth gradients may show bands, edges may develop halos, and textured details can become blocky. The result can still be useful for a website or message, but it is not a byte-for-byte copy of the source.

What lossless compression preserves

Lossless encoding reconstructs the decoded pixel values exactly when the same pixel format and color interpretation are maintained. PNG uses lossless compression; WebP also offers a lossless mode. Lossless does not mean every file has identical metadata, color profiles or sizes after conversion. It also does not promise the output will be smaller: images with noise or irregular patterns may resist compression.

A reproducible comparison instead of a magic percentage

Choose one source photograph and one simple graphic. Keep the originals. Export the photograph as a high-quality JPEG, then at a more aggressive setting, and compare output byte counts and critical image details. Separately, save a flat-color graphic as PNG. An example number shown in an illustration is not a benchmark of Every Tiny Tool. Actual compression ratios depend on the image, export settings, encoder and whether resizing occurs.

Why saving a lossy image repeatedly matters

An already-compressed JPEG may acquire additional artifacts when re-encoded with another lossy save. This is especially easy to spot around small text, foliage and contrasting edges. Preserve the original or a lossless editing master, and make final delivery versions from that master whenever possible. Merely copying an existing JPEG file does not cause generation loss; re-encoding does.

Which format fits which job

Photographic images often benefit from JPEG or lossy WebP. Diagrams, logos, screenshots and transparency-heavy assets often benefit from PNG or a carefully chosen lossless format. WebP may be used with either compression method, so the filename extension alone does not tell you whether its content is lossy. Inspect a sample at the size people will actually see it.

A simple quality-check workflow

Check the source dimensions, choose one variable to change, export, then inspect the output next to the original at 100% scale. Record the exact file size and file format. Examine gradients, thin text and textured areas before choosing the smaller export. For critical source artwork, archive the original separately from delivery files.

Frequently asked questions

Does lossless always make files smaller?

No. The compression ratio depends on content and encoder; some files may barely shrink.

Does WebP always mean lossy compression?

No. WebP supports both lossy and lossless encoding.

Does copying JPEG reduce its quality?

Copying bytes does not; a new lossy re-encode can.
